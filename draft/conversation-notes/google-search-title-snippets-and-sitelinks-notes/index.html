Google Search Titles, Snippets, and Sitelinks Notes

source/_drafts/conversation-notes/google-search-title-snippets-and-sitelinks-notes.md

Question

  • I noticed that Google search results can show a title different from the page content.
  • I compared another blog with my own site.
  • The other blog showed a stable brand-like title:
Wiwi.Blog: Wiwi's blog
  • My site showed the homepage banner phrase:
Do. Or do not.
  • I wanted to understand:
    • why Google shows different titles
    • why Google extracts page content into the search result
    • why some sites get extra links under the result

Search Result Title Is Not Always the HTML Title

  • A web page has multiple title-like signals.
  • Google may choose from several sources.
  • The most important sources are:
    • the <title> tag
    • the page h1
    • Open Graph title
    • visible page text
    • anchor text from links pointing to the page

HTML Title

  • The HTML title lives in the page <head>.
  • Example:
<title>My Blog Title</title>
  • This is the normal SEO title.
  • Google often uses it.
  • But Google does not guarantee that it will use it exactly.

H1 Title

  • The page h1 is the visible main heading.
  • Example:
<h1>Do. Or do not.</h1>
  • If the HTML title is weak, too short, too long, or unclear, Google may prefer the h1.
  • If the h1 is more readable, Google may use it in the search result.

Open Graph Title

  • Open Graph title is usually for social sharing.
  • Example:
<meta property="og:title" content="My Blog Title" />
  • Facebook, LINE, Twitter, and other platforms use this metadata.
  • Google may also consider it as another title signal.

Why Google Rewrites Titles

  • Google wants the search result to be useful for the searcher.

  • Google may rewrite the title when:

    • the <title> is too short
    • the <title> is too long
    • the <title> is generic
    • the <title> looks like keyword stuffing
    • the title does not match the page content
    • the title does not match the search intent

  • The title is a strong suggestion.

  • It is not a forced instruction.

Wiwi.Blog Case

  • The search result title looked like:
Wiwi.Blog: Wiwi's blog

  • This is a brand-first title.

  • It looks like a combination of:

    • site title
    • site description

  • Many Jekyll-style sites use config values like:

title: Wiwi.Blog
description: Wiwi's blog
  • The layout may combine them into:
site.title + ": " + site.description
  • This creates a stable homepage title.
  • Google can understand it as a site identity.

My Homepage Case

  • My homepage hero title is:
Do. Or do not.

  • It is meaningful as a banner phrase.

  • It keeps the old blog mood.

  • But as a search result title, it can look like a standalone article title.

  • It does not directly say:

    • who owns the site
    • what the site is about
    • why this is a blog homepage

  • This is why Google or the browser may surface it as the page title.

  • The site identity is weaker than the hero phrase.

Site Title Strategy

  • A stable homepage title should usually be brand-first.
  • A content phrase can still appear in the hero.
  • But the SEO title should explain the site.

Possible structure:

Hsufit.dev: A nest for an engineer
  • The hero can still say:
Do. Or do not.
There is no try.
  • This separates:
    • SEO identity
    • visual identity
    • homepage mood

Search Snippet

  • The search result is not a direct copy of the page.
  • Google builds a search snippet.
  • A snippet may include:
    • rewritten title
    • page URL
    • meta description
    • visible page text
    • text near the matched keyword

Where the Description Comes From

  • Google may use:

    • <meta name="description">
    • homepage intro text
    • paragraph text
    • nearby content around the query match

  • If the meta description is missing or too generic, Google may generate its own.

  • If the homepage text is clearer than the meta description, Google may use visible text.

Why Google Listed Wiwi.Blog Content

  • The search result showed:

    • a brand title
    • a short homepage description
    • extra section links

  • These pieces likely came from different signals.

  • The title came from title or site identity signals.

  • The description came from readable homepage text.

  • The extra links came from site structure.

Sitelinks

  • The extra links under a Google search result are sitelinks.
  • They are generated by Google automatically.
  • They are not manually written in a meta tag.

Examples:

/now
/docs
/posts
/notes
  • Google may show sitelinks when it understands the site structure.

What Helps Sitelinks Appear

  • Clear navigation helps.

  • Clear internal links help.

  • Stable page titles help.

  • Meaningful page headings help.

  • A sitemap helps discovery.

  • A trusted and crawled site helps.

  • New sites may not get sitelinks immediately.

  • Google needs time and enough signals.

Why My Site May Not Show Rich Results Yet

  • The homepage may have too little descriptive text.
  • The hero phrase is strong visually but weak as a search title.
  • The site may not have enough indexed pages yet.
  • The internal structure may not be trusted by Google yet.
  • The sitemap and indexing flow may still be new.
  • The site may not have many external links yet.

Practical Improvements

  • Keep SITE.title stable.
  • Keep SITE.desc descriptive.
  • Make the HTML <title> brand-first.
  • Make each page title unique.
  • Keep h1 close to the page topic.
  • Add a useful meta description.
  • Add a short homepage intro.
  • Keep navigation clear.
  • Link important pages from the homepage.
  • Submit the sitemap to Google Search Console.
  • Request indexing for the homepage.

AstroPaper Mapping

  • SITE.title controls the default site title.

  • SITE.desc controls the default description.

  • Layout.astro emits:

    • <title>
    • meta description
    • Open Graph title
    • Twitter title

  • The homepage visual hero is in:

astro-paper-hsufit/src/pages/index.astro
  • The site-level SEO config is in:
astro-paper-hsufit/src/config.ts
  • The layout-level metadata is in:
astro-paper-hsufit/src/layouts/Layout.astro

Design Choice

  • Keep the old banner phrase on the homepage.
  • Do not rely on the hero phrase as the whole site identity.
  • Use a brand-style site title for SEO.
  • Use a clear site description for snippets.
  • Use navigation and sitemap to help Google understand the site.

Final Summary

  • Google search results are generated from multiple page signals.
  • Google may rewrite titles.
  • Google may generate snippets from visible content.
  • Sitelinks are generated from site structure.
  • Do. Or do not. is good as a hero phrase.
  • It is weaker as a search result title.
  • A blog homepage should make the brand and purpose clear.
  • Better site identity helps both users and search engines.
